How Many Solar Panels Do I Need? 3 kW, 5 kW and 10 kW Systems Explained

3 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

Solar systems are sold by kilowatt (kW), but what you actually buy is a number of panels. Here is how to go from the electricity you use to the panels on your roof.

Step 1: How big a system do you need?

In most of India, each 1 kW of panels generates roughly 4 to 5 units (kWh) a day on average across the year - more in sunny summer months, less during the monsoon. So:

  • A 3 kW system makes about 12-15 units a day, or 360-450 units a month.
  • A 5 kW system makes about 20-25 units a day, or 600-750 units a month.
  • A 10 kW system makes about 40-50 units a day, or 1,200-1,500 units a month.

Check your electricity bill for monthly units and pick the size that covers most of them. Our solar calculator does this for you.

Step 2: Count the panels

Number of panels = system size (W) / panel wattage, rounded up. With PowerRun mono PERC panels:

System550 Wp panels / 600 Wp panels
3 kW6 panels (3.3 kWp) / 5 panels (3.0 kWp)
5 kW10 panels (5.5 kWp) / 9 panels (5.4 kWp)
10 kW19 panels (10.45 kWp) / 17 panels (10.2 kWp)

Higher-wattage panels mean fewer modules, fewer mounting points and less wiring for the same output.

Step 3: Check your roof space

Plan for roughly 80-100 sq ft of shade-free roof per kW, which leaves room for spacing and walkways. A 5 kW system therefore needs around 400-500 sq ft. Avoid spots shaded by water tanks, trees or neighbouring buildings - even partial shade cuts output.

Step 4: Match the panels to your inverter

Two limits on the inverter matter:

  • Maximum PV input power. The PowerRun 3.6 kW hybrid inverter accepts up to 5,000 W of panels, the 6.2 kW up to 8,500 W, and the 10.2 kW and 12 kW models up to 2 x 8,500 W across two MPPT inputs.
  • PV input voltage range. Panels connected in series add up their voltages, and the string must stay inside the inverter's range (60-500 V DC on the 3.6 kW to 6.2 kW models). Panel voltage rises on cold mornings, so leave a margin.

Adding a battery
