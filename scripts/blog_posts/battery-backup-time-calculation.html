"How long will it run?" is the first question everyone asks about an inverter battery. The answer comes from one simple formula - no guesswork needed.

The formula

Backup hours = battery energy (kWh) x usable fraction x inverter efficiency / load (kW)

  • Battery energy = voltage x amp-hours / 1000. A 51.2 V 100 Ah battery holds 51.2 x 100 / 1000 = 5.12 kWh.
  • Usable fraction - about 0.8 for LiFePO4 lithium (rated at 80% depth of discharge), about 0.5 for lead-acid.
  • Inverter efficiency - use about 0.9 to allow for conversion losses.
  • Load - the total watts running, divided by 1000.

Example 1: a PowerRun 51.2 V 100 Ah lithium battery

5.12 kWh x 0.8 x 0.9 = about 3.7 kWh delivered.

  • At 500 W (lights, fans, TV, router): 3.7 / 0.5 = about 7 hours
  • At 800 W (add a fridge): 3.7 / 0.8 = about 4.5 hours
  • At 2 kW (add a 1.5 ton AC): 3.7 / 2 = under 2 hours

The 25.6 V 200 Ah battery stores the same 5.12 kWh, so it gives the same backup on a 24 V inverter.

Example 2: a 12 V 150 Ah lead-acid battery

12 x 150 / 1000 = 1.8 kWh. Usable: 1.8 x 0.5 x 0.9 = about 0.8 kWh. At 500 W that is roughly 1.6 hours - which is why lead-acid systems need several batteries for long cuts.

Working backwards: what capacity do I need?

Flip the formula: battery kWh needed = load (kW) x hours / (0.8 x 0.9) for lithium.

For 800 W over 6 hours: 0.8 x 6 / 0.72 = 6.7 kWh. The nearest PowerRun options are the 25.6 V 280 Ah (7.17 kWh) for a 24 V inverter or the 51.2 V 200 Ah (10.24 kWh) for a 48 V inverter.

Three tips for longer backup

  • Switch to BLDC fans and LED lights. A BLDC fan uses less than half the power of a regular fan.
  • Keep heavy loads off backup. Geysers, irons and induction cooktops drain a battery in minutes.
  • Add solar. With a hybrid inverter, the sun recharges the battery during the day, so it starts every evening full.
